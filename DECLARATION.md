# A DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE
## From Corporate Dominance and Overreach

**PARALLAX CONGRESS**

*AI & Technology*

### The Declaration

When the institutions entrusted with advancing human progress become instruments of concentrated power, it becomes necessary for free people to reconsider the terms upon which they participate in the economy and the technologies that increasingly govern their lives.

A decent respect for the public requires that we declare our reasons.

We hold that the right to innovate, to compete, to create, and to participate in economic life belongs to all people.

That enterprise and invention are among the great engines of human prosperity.

That private property, free markets, and the pursuit of commercial success are essential to a free society.

That these freedoms cannot flourish where competition is suppressed, access is controlled, and the opportunities of the many are subordinated to the interests of the few.

And that no corporation, regardless of its wealth, influence, or technological achievements, possesses an inherent right to determine the future of humanity.

### Our Grievances

The history of modern technology is one of extraordinary human achievement. It is also increasingly a history of economic concentration.

Technologies developed through generations of public research, private investment, academic discovery, and individual ingenuity have become essential foundations of modern civilization.

Yet control over these foundations has increasingly accumulated among a small number of powerful corporations.

Let the following grievances be submitted to the judgment of the public:

**They have erected barriers to competition**, employing their enormous financial and technological advantages to reinforce their dominance and discourage independent alternatives.

**They have cultivated dependence**, binding businesses, governments, developers, and individuals to proprietary ecosystems whose costs and restrictions they may alter with little meaningful negotiation.

**They have accumulated extraordinary influence over public policy**, wielding financial resources and institutional access unavailable to ordinary citizens and smaller competitors.

**They have treated information as an instrument of control**, collecting and commercializing the activities, preferences, and creations of individuals on a scale previously unimaginable.

**They have acquired, restricted, or displaced competing innovations**, too often protecting existing markets rather than permitting new ones to flourish.

**They have confused corporate efficiency with economic progress**, pursuing optimization within their own institutions while imposing costs, dependencies, and limitations upon the broader economy.

**They have promoted technological dependence as inevitable**, presenting corporate control over essential infrastructure as the necessary price of innovation.

**They have exercised private authority over increasingly public spaces**, making consequential decisions about communication, commerce, information, and access without corresponding democratic accountability.

These grievances do not condemn every large enterprise, nor every exercise of corporate power. They identify practices and structures that deserve public scrutiny, competitive challenge, and, where justified, legal remedy.

### On Competition and Liberty

We reject the proposition that technological progress requires the permanent dominance of a privileged few.

We recognize that scale can produce genuine efficiencies, that corporations can serve the public good, and that private enterprise has contributed immeasurably to human prosperity.

We do not seek to destroy these achievements.

We seek to preserve the conditions that make them possible.

Competition disciplines power. It rewards innovation, exposes waste, creates opportunity, and gives consumers and businesses the freedom to choose.

Where competition is obstructed, markets cease to serve their full purpose.

Where essential infrastructure becomes inaccessible to independent participants, innovation suffers.

Where economic power becomes sufficient to dictate the terms of public life, liberty itself is diminished.

The answer is to restore meaningful competition and the freedom to participate.

### Our Declaration

We, the founding members and supporters of **Parallax Congress**, therefore declare our independence from the presumption that the future of artificial intelligence and technology must be determined by the corporations that presently dominate them.

We affirm the right of individuals and organizations to develop, own, operate, repair, improve, and exchange technology.

We affirm the importance of open standards, interoperability, portability, and independent infrastructure.

We affirm the right of entrepreneurs and innovators to compete without unjust exclusion or artificial barriers.

We support the enforcement of fair competition laws, the protection of fundamental rights, and public oversight where private power threatens the public interest.

We recognize artificial intelligence as a transformative achievement whose benefits and risks demand broad public participation in its governance.

We declare that economic influence shall not determine an individual's voice within our Congress.

**One person. One vote.**

Through open deliberation, democratic proposals, public voting, and transparent recommendations, we establish Parallax Congress as a forum through which people may help shape the future of AI and technology.

Through voluntary contributions and paid advisory participation, we shall finance the research, education, and institutional work necessary to advance this mission, without selling additional voting power.

Through **Fervent Wishes Co.**, our designated advocacy partner, we shall pursue lawful public advocacy, coalition building, and legislative engagement in support of policies adopted through our democratic processes.

And through competition, independent enterprise, and technological innovation, we shall seek to demonstrate the alternatives we advocate.

### To the Corporations of the World

We do not deny your achievements, your contributions, or your right to prosper.

We ask that you recognize the same rights in others.

Your investments do not entitle you to permanent markets.

Your innovations do not entitle you to suppress those that follow.

Your ownership does not exempt you from accountability.

Your influence does not supersede the sovereignty of the people or the authority of their democratic institutions.

We invite you to compete, to collaborate, and to participate under principles that respect the equal standing of others.

We welcome your expertise and your contributions.

We shall not surrender our independence in exchange for them.

### Our Commitment

We understand that concentrated power will not be overcome by declarations alone.

The work will require investment, organization, research, public participation, entrepreneurship, and sustained advocacy.

We accept that responsibility.

We shall build institutions capable of enduring beyond their founders.

We shall encourage competition even when it challenges our own interests.

We shall subject our proposals to public scrutiny and our leadership to accountability.

And we shall preserve the freedom of future generations to challenge the institutions we ourselves create.

For no institution deserves an exemption from the principles it professes.

We therefore publish this Declaration as an invitation to individuals, enterprises, researchers, developers, and communities throughout the world.

Let those who believe in a competitive, democratic, and independent technological future join us.

**We declare our independence from corporate dominance and overreach.**

And for the advancement of these principles, we pledge our knowledge, our resources, our labor, and our good faith.
